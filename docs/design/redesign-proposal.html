Steel, as a crypto desk would build it.

The dashboard was laid out for stocks and then had a crypto terminal bolted on: seven views that grew by accretion, a light/dark toggle that now wraps, and no identity of its own. This is a proposal to reorganize it around what a crypto trader looks at and in what order, to give Steel a visual system that is not Flint's, and to add a public page and a whitepaper that read differently from Flint's.

The thesis

Steel is the desk's second opinion: it watches every venue around the clock, forecasts the next hour as a distribution, shows its work, pays every real cost in simulation, and never trades unless armed. The interface should be built on the trader's routine, not on where the code lives.

What changes

Views regrouped as Desk, Chart, Flow, Book, Model, Venues, Ops. A cold-steel identity with one accent and a heat scale taken from tempering colours. A public page that opens on the live tape. A whitepaper organised around measurement and cost honesty.

What stays

The engine, the API, the panels as widgets, the chart engine, the seven-key navigation. Nothing here changes a forecast or a fee; it changes where things sit and how they look.

The terminals

Market Monkey: footprint, book heatmap, TPO, CVD, liquidations, dockable widgets, saved layouts, scripting. Coinglass: the free liquidation heatmap and OI/funding tables, derivatives-first, little spot. Velo: derivatives charting with deep history and a clean chart. Laevitas: options-first, institutional. Bookmap and TradingLite: book heatmap on the chart with footprint clusters. aggr.trade: open-source multi-venue tape with audio and large-trade grouping.

None of them forecasts. None of them keeps a paper book that pays fees, funding and on-chain transfer waits. None of them says how its liquidation map was made or scores it against the real forced orders. That is the identity.

The visual conventions

Dark, number-first, hairline-separated dense panels, tabular figures so values do not shift as they update, green for longs and red for shorts always paired with a label, one accent for live data and primary actions. Hyperliquid's reading of this is a blue-shifted near-black canvas with a single mint accent and white text at stepped opacities. Light themes exist mainly for bright rooms and screenshots.

Steel follows the conventions and differs in the accent and the heat scale, which are its own.

Identity: cold steel, one accent, a heat scale

Flint is warm: a warm black, fire orange, gold, a system sans. Steel is its cold sibling. The canvas is a blue-black; the accent is tempered-steel blue; and the one detail only this subject has is the tempering scale, the oxide colours steel takes as it heats (straw, bronze, purple, blue), which becomes the heat scale for every map and the pulse under the wordmark.

STEEL

The wordmark is Archivo at its narrow width, wide-tracked. The temper line under it is the live-data pulse: it drifts while feeds tick and freezes on a stall. Tagline: the market never closes; neither does the model.

The views, regrouped by what a trader does

Seven views stay (the keys 1 to 7 work the same), but they are renamed and regrouped so each one answers one question. Every panel remains a widget: drag, collapse, export.

The status strip

One row on every view: the routine's numbers (next event in h, funding median, OI 1h, liquidations 1h by side, breadth, F&G, dislocations open, trusted or not). It is the 15-minute checklist made permanent.

The pair rail

Tape, book, derivatives and options for the selected pair live beside the chart, not in a modal. Selecting a pair anywhere (screener, calls, book) moves the rail and the chart with it; the link is on by default.

The phone

Desk and the alerts work at phone width: the strip stacks, the heatmap goes four wide, the calls list is the page. The rest stays desktop.

The public page

Flint's page explains: what it does, how it works, run it. Steel's should show: it opens on the market itself, and the sections follow the trader's day. Hosted like Flint's, from docs/ on GitHub Pages, at steel.thingg.co (a CNAME beside Flint's flint.thingg.co).

Structure

  • Hero. The wordmark, the temper line, and under it a live-looking tape: a canvas replaying a bundled minute of real prints from six venues, with the forecast fan drawn ahead. Headline: The market never closes. Neither does the model. Two buttons: run it, read the paper.
  • 07:00 UTC. The routine as sections: the clock, the funding sweep, open interest, the liquidation map, CVD, the levels, who is positioned where. Each with one real screenshot and one sentence on where the number comes from.
  • It shows its work. Four claims, each a rule in the code: every map says how it was made; every simulated dollar pays real fees, funding and transfer waits; keys are read-only; nothing is sent unless armed, and the kill switch refuses everything.
  • What it is not. Not advice, not a signal service, not a hosted product. A research toy that runs on your box.
  • Beside the terminals. A short table: footprint, heatmaps, scripting, alerts, API, MCP, and the three things only Steel has.
  • Run it. The uv commands, the Spark service, the token, the data API, the MCP server.

How it differs from Flint's

  • Flint leads with a screenshot; Steel leads with motion (the tape) because the subject is a market that never stops.
  • Flint's sections are about the system; Steel's are about the trader's hours.
  • Flint is warm and explanatory; Steel is cold and declarative: numbers first, every claim with its source.
  • Flint has one page and a paper; Steel adds a live status strip at the top of the page fed by a small JSON the box publishes (optional, off by default), so the page can show "trusted since", "venues feeding", "last backtest" without exposing the dashboard.

The whitepaper

Steel's current paper mirrors Flint's outline (problem, data, model, learning, calibration, policy, pricing, evaluation, system). The proposal is to reorganise it around the two things that make Steel different: it measures a market that never closes from many venues at once, and it refuses to pretend. The outline:

  1. A market that never closes. What changes: no session, no overnight gap, a UTC day that is only a bookkeeping line, a stall that is always an alarm.
  2. Measuring the market. The tape (every print from every venue), the books (one dollar class at a time), the derivatives feeds (OI, funding, liquidations, four venues), the options surface, the calendar, the prediction markets. What is measured and what is modeled.
  3. From measurement to features. The 40 columns; the horizon axis; why a feature-set change resets the net and keeps the replay.
  4. Learning around the clock. Warmup, the held-out guard, the idle trainer, the weekly backtest in the thinnest tape, the GPU shared with Flint.
  5. Trust. The held-out scorecard, the live labels, calibration by pair, what "trusted" means and when it is withdrawn.
  6. Every cost is real. Venue fee profiles, funding by side, the half-spread, impact and Amihud, on-chain transfers with their fees and waits, bounded risk by construction (the isolated-margin short).
  7. Cross-venue structure. Dislocations and their windows, identity, the impossible-gap rule, the arbitrage replay by latency rung.
  8. Honest maps. The modeled liquidation map and its calibration against the venues' real forced orders; the book heatmap's method.
  9. From paper to live. The promotion checklist, the gate, the algorithms, the dead-man rule, the kill switch.
  10. Evaluation. The backtest's totals and the first paper weeks, reported with their caveats.
  11. Limitations. Vendor data, survivorship in the universe, the simulator's history, what the net cannot see.
  12. Future work. Pairs as a conversion graph (K20), real exogenous history, the model's own features.

Different from Flint's in kind

  • Each section opens with a measurement from a real run and its date (the dislocation day, the four-venue OI, the calibration score once there are liquidations to score). Flint's paper argues from design; Steel's argues from the log.
  • A "modeled / measured" margin tag on every figure and table.
  • The same type and tokens as the dashboard and the public page, so the three read as one product.
  • An appendix of every API route, every feature column and every setting, generated from the code at build time so it cannot drift.

Plan

1 · identity

Tokens, type, wordmark, the temper line; the header fixed. The dashboard's palette and fonts move to the new tokens (one stylesheet change; every panel inherits), the theme toggle and the Layout menu stop wrapping, the wordmark and the pulse land in the header. About a day. Visible at once on the smoke instance.

2 · public page

docs/index.html with the live-tape hero, the routine sections, the honesty section and the run-it section, plus the CNAME and the Pages workflow. Screenshots rendered from the restyled dashboard. One to two days.

3 · views

Desk, Chart with the rail, Flow, Model. Panels move between views (they are widgets already), the status strip becomes global, the pair detail folds into Chart, the derivatives panels gather on Flow. Old hashes keep working through the legacy map. Two to three days.

4 · whitepaper

The restructured paper with run data in every section and the generated appendix. One to two days, after the views settle so the screenshots are final.

5 · phone

Desk and alerts at phone width, a web-app manifest, and the sound and notification toggles that make a phone useful. One day.

Decisions needed before building

The accent. Tempered blue as proposed, or something further from the crowd (the straw as the accent and blue as heat would invert the scale and read warmer).
The names. Desk / Chart / Flow / Book / Model / Venues / Ops, or keep Market and Research as names people already know.
The domain. steel.thingg.co beside flint.thingg.co, and whether the page's optional live strip (a small JSON the box publishes) is wanted at all.
The order. Identity first (fast, visible, no behaviour change) and the public page second, then the views; or views first so the public page's screenshots show the final layout.

Answer these four and the build starts with the identity on the running smoke instance, so each step can be checked in a browser as it lands.
